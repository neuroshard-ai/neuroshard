## Question

> Can independent operators discover work, acquire assigned shards, join,
> leave and replace unavailable providers through the protocol, while no
> operator controls one third or more of consensus voting power, and while
> the accepted graph keeps serving?

Separate keys, availability zones or wallets under one administrator do not
answer this. The protocol sees keys, not operators. The soak therefore names
operators explicitly, aggregates voting power by administrator, and refuses to
count this operator's AWS account as more than one of them.

## Two frozen stages

1. **CPU protocol preflight.** Four genesis validators with equal bonds. Each
   validator's voting share is strictly less than one third (four equal shares
   of 1/4 pass; three equal shares of 1/3 fail). A key that is not in genesis
   can receive a native transfer, register as a provider and advertise a rank
   of the accepted graph without SSH. One provider key still cannot hold the
   complete backbone. This stage executes no neural tensors and does not claim
   independent administration.

2. **Independent-operator soak.** Only after stage 1. At least four
   independently administered operators. Voting concentration is computed by
   administrator: four equal keys under three administrators (2+1+1) fail,
   because one administrator holds half the power. This operator may hold at
   most one of the four validators. Demonstrate join, leave,
   required-backbone-shard loss, coordinator loss, state recovery and continued
   service. No GPU is authorized by this specification.

## What this specification does not do

It does not launch GPUs. It does not promote a model. It does not reopen the
original 128-task programming final. It does not treat extra AWS instances as
independent operators. It does not replace the 0.4.0 genesis. Object mirrors
remain a convenience, not independence. Checklist item 4 stays open until the
soak's independent-administration criterion is met.
